Your professional identity develops through the questions you ask, the decisions you explain and the way you work with people. In ITBA, these three identities express the kind of professional we want to help you become.

## Change Agent

**Understand the need. Help people move forward responsibly.**

Start with curiosity about what people are trying to achieve. Investigate what is happening, consider who a change would affect, and explore options before recommending a solution. Your contribution is helping people understand why a change matters and what value it could create.

<p className="identity-prompt">In practice, that means asking: <em>What problem are we trying to solve? What evidence supports our explanation? How would we know things had improved?</em></p>

[Explore the cause-and-effect exemplar](/exemplars/#causes) to see how possible explanations can become questions to investigate.

Our Change Agent identity is informed by [IIBA’s explanation of business analysis](https://www.iiba.org/professional-development/career-centre/what-is-business-analysis/).

## Trusted Advisor

**Listen carefully. Use evidence. Follow through.**

Trust develops through how you work with others. We encourage you to understand someone’s needs before offering advice, make your reasoning clear, acknowledge uncertainty and keep your commitments. A useful recommendation considers the people who must live with the decision.

<p className="identity-prompt">In practice, that means asking: <em>Whose perspective is missing? What do I need to understand before advising? Can others see how I reached this recommendation?</em></p>

[Explore the stakeholder exemplar](/exemplars/#stakeholders) to see how different needs, influence and communication preferences shape the work.

This identity draws on David H. Maister, Robert M. Galford and Charles H. Green’s [The Trusted Advisor](https://trustedadvisor.com/books/the-trusted-advisor).

## Information Engineer

**Bring analytical depth and connect across disciplines.**

Business analysis is the core of this identity. Alongside that depth, we value the technical and business understanding that helps you collaborate across specialties. You learn to organize information, make relationships visible and translate between a business need and a discussion about possible solutions.

<p className="identity-prompt">In practice, that means asking: <em>How does this fit together? What information crosses the boundary? Can a business stakeholder and a technical teammate understand the same model?</em></p>

[Explore the context diagram](/exemplars/#context) and [requirements exemplars](/exemplars/#requirements) to see those connections made explicit.
