You bring your goals, experience and curiosity to ITBA. We want to help you turn them into purposeful learning: a plan you can act on, knowledge you can use again, and work you can reflect on with confidence.

We’ve designed an **ITBA Student Dashboard template in Notion** to support that process. It gives you a starting point for your own knowledge management system—one you can customize as your studies and interests develop.

## Give your studies a direction

What brings you to ITBA? What do you want to be able to do? What might make progress difficult?

Use your workspace to think through those questions and turn your intentions into practical actions. A useful strategy connects your purpose with the habits, priorities and support you need along the way.

**Try this:** choose one learning goal, identify a small next step, and decide when you’ll review your progress.

## Build knowledge you can return to

Make room for the ideas, sources, questions and examples that matter to your learning. Connect a new concept with something you have already studied or tried. Record where information came from so you can return to its source.

**Try this:** after a class or project session, capture one useful idea, one connection and one question you still want to explore.

## Develop a portfolio of growth

Your work can help tell the story of how you think. Keep selected examples and explain the need, your contribution, the choices you made and what you learned from feedback. Revisit earlier work so you can recognize how your understanding has changed.

You might choose a process model, a stakeholder analysis, a set of requirements or a project reflection. The value comes from explaining the work and your learning—not simply collecting files.

**Try this:** select one artifact and write a short account of the problem, your role, your reasoning and what you would improve next time. Use work you have permission to share, and credit collaborators.

[Browse the exemplars](/exemplars/) for examples of the kinds of analysis you could discuss in a portfolio.

## Review, adjust and keep going

Your strategy should develop with you. Set aside time to review what is working, where you need help and what deserves attention next. Use your notes and examples to make conversations about your progress more concrete.

These habits connect directly with [our professional identities](/our-identity/): purposeful change, thoughtful relationships and clear information.
