Maybe you’re the person who explains the process to new staff. Or the one who notices where requests get stuck, translates between the front line and the technical team, or asks why things are done a certain way. Those habits are the beginnings of business analysis.

IT Business Analysis at NAIT helps you build on that experience: understand a need before proposing a change, make information clear enough for others to act on, and work confidently between business and technology.

## Recognize what you already do

Our three professional identities describe the kind of contribution a business analyst makes. Many working professionals recognize part of their current role in at least one of them.

<ul className="touchpoints">
  <li><a href="/our-identity/#change-agent"><strong>Change Agent</strong><span>You look for what would make a process better for the people in it.</span></a></li>
  <li><a href="/our-identity/#trusted-advisor"><strong>Trusted Advisor</strong><span>Colleagues come to you because you listen and explain options clearly.</span></a></li>
  <li><a href="/our-identity/#information-engineer"><strong>Information Engineer</strong><span>You bring order to messy information so others can work with it.</span></a></li>
</ul>

**Try this:** think of a recent change at work that didn’t go as planned. What question, asked earlier, might have changed the outcome?

## See the tools of the trade

Business analysts make knowledge visible. The exemplars show the assets you’ll learn to create, and how each one helps a team see, question and act on what it knows.
