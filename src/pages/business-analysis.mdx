## The starting point

A campus club has trouble booking meeting rooms. Members send messages, wait for replies and sometimes discover that their preferred room is unavailable. Someone suggests building a new booking app.

A business analyst starts by understanding the problem and the people involved.

## 1. Listen to the people involved

Talk with club members and the people managing room bookings. Ask them to describe a recent experience:

- What were they trying to do?
- Which part was difficult or uncertain?
- What information did they need, and when?
- What already worked well?

Different people may experience the same process differently. A slow reply, for example, could reflect missing information, an unclear approval step or a room that needs special preparation.

## 2. Make the process visible

Map the steps from requesting a room to receiving confirmation. Identify where information is missing, where people wait and who makes each decision.

The point is to build a shared understanding. A simple process diagram can help people check whether the analyst has understood what actually happens.

## 3. Explore options before choosing a solution

A new application is one possible response. Other options might include clearer booking instructions, a consistent request format or better visibility of room availability.

Compare the options against the needs you discovered, the effort involved and the people affected. Then define what a useful improvement would look like—for example, fewer incomplete requests or less uncertainty about a booking’s status.

## Where technology fits

If software is part of the response, the analyst helps make the requirements clear enough for designers and developers to work with. That includes describing what people need to do, what information the system needs and how the team will check that the result meets the need.
